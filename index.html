About

About me.

I'm an applied economist and Assistant Professor of Agribusiness Management at the State University of New York at Cobleskill. I hold a Ph.D. in Applied Economics, with a minor in Statistics, from the University of Florida, and an M.S. in Agricultural and Resource Economics from the University of Delaware.

My research examines how people, firms, and institutions respond to economic incentives, information, and policy — with a focus on food-safety regulation, market signals, disclosure policy, and consumer decision-making. I work primarily with applied econometrics and experimental methods, including difference-in-differences, synthetic control, discrete choice experiments, and behavioral models, along with tools like eye-tracking to study decision processes.

Outside of research, I enjoy hiking, traveling, and cooking while listening to music.

03

Marketing analytics · Bayesian modeling

Marketing Mix Modeling (MMM) with Bayesian Inference

Built a Bayesian MMM using Google Meridian to estimate channel-level contribution and optimize a $220M media budget, identifying a reallocation strategy that lifted ROI from 0.7 to 0.8 — with Channel 2 emerging as the most efficient channel (ROI 2.02).
