Suraj Gurung

Applied Economist · Causal Inference · Experimentation · Machine Learning

I am an applied economist with a Ph.D. in Applied Economics and a minor in Statistics from the University of Florida. My work combines causal inference, experimental design, econometrics, and machine learning to study consumer behavior, marketplace decisions, and policy interventions.

I work with experimental, survey, administrative, and large-scale observational data to quantify behavior, evaluate interventions, and translate statistical evidence into decisions. My research spans consumer choice, food and marketplace policy, marketing analytics, and behavioral economics.

I am currently an Assistant Professor of Agribusiness Management at SUNY Cobleskill, where I teach agricultural economics and conduct research on consumer decision-making and market behavior.

Featured Projects

Causal Inference, Experimentation & Applied Economics

Consumer Choice & Market Signal Analysis

Designed and analyzed a discrete choice experiment with more than 1,200 consumers to estimate willingness-to-pay for food safety, regulatory oversight, private credentials, and market signals. Mixed logit models quantified heterogeneous consumer preferences and substitution patterns.

Discrete Choice Experiment Mixed Logit Experimental Design Willingness-to-Pay

Policy Evaluation & Compliance Analytics

Built a multi-city restaurant inspection dataset to estimate the causal effect of a food safety disclosure policy. Difference-in-differences, event-study, and synthetic control methods indicated an approximately 15% reduction in violations after implementation.

Difference-in-Differences Event Study Synthetic Control Python

Marketing Mix Modeling with Bayesian Inference

Built a Bayesian marketing mix model using Google Meridian to estimate channel-level contribution and evaluate allocation of a modeled $220 million media budget. Scenario analysis identified a reallocation associated with approximately $4 million in additional modeled incremental revenue.

Bayesian Modeling Marketing Analytics MCMC Google Meridian

Behavioral Measurement & Decision Pathways

Combined the Theory of Planned Behavior with PLS-SEM, necessary condition analysis, and configurational analysis to study adoption of an emerging food marketplace. The analysis examined how attitudes, social norms, perceived control, trust, benefits, and risk jointly shape purchase intentions.

PLS-SEM NCA fsQCA Behavioral Economics

Data Science & Machine Learning

Credit Risk Modeling →

Developed binary classification models for loan default prediction using more than 12,000 observations. Evaluated model discrimination and tuned classification thresholds to balance precision and recall.

Python scikit-learn Logistic Regression Decision Trees ROC/AUC

Implied Volatility Modeling →

Developed a neural network model for daily changes in S&P 500 call-option implied volatility using delta, time-to-maturity, market returns, and approximately two million observations.

TensorFlow Neural Networks Financial Data Large-Scale Modeling

Portfolio Modeling with Autoencoders →

Applied autoencoder-based representation learning to Nasdaq-100 return data to examine latent return structure and support portfolio construction experiments.

TensorFlow Keras Autoencoders Representation Learning

Investment Risk Segmentation →

Applied K-means clustering to macroeconomic and financial indicators to segment countries into investment risk groups. The number of clusters was evaluated using silhouette scores.

scikit-learn K-Means Clustering Model Selection
